Japan Studies 216 (Culture)
Japanese Cultural Patterns: A Cross-Cultural Perspective
Kevin Ottoson(ｹﾋﾞﾝ ｵｯﾄｿﾝ)
This course examines salient Japanese cultural patterns that are often diﬃcult to recognize and articulate explicitly.
Through the study of conventional principles and everyday practices in contemporary Japan, particularly those not
typically presented in media or standard textbooks, students will explore how cultural patterns shape perception,
behavior, and social interaction.
By engaging with Japanese culture from a cross cultural perspective, students will develop a deeper understanding of
how cultural values inﬂuence actions, communication styles, and social expectations. The course also emphasizes
critical reﬂection, encouraging students to examine cultural generalizations and consider the diversity and complexity
within Japanese society. Through readings, structured discussion, and a small scale ethnographic research project,
students will strengthen their intercultural awareness and analytical skills.
Course Objectives
Through this course, students will develop intercultural competence and research skills. By the end of the course,
students should be able to:
① Demonstrate knowledge of key Japanese cultural patterns and orientations.
② Explain how cultural values inﬂuence observable behavior in contemporary Japan.
③ Critically examine cultural generalizations and recognize cultural complexity.
④ Reﬂect on their own cultural positioning and identity.
⑤ Conduct and present a small scale ethnographic interview using appropriate research practices.
Class Preparation
Students are expected to spend approximately 180 minutes per week preparing for class. Preparation includes assigned
readings, written responses, research tasks, and preparation for discussion activities. Mini lectures, guided discussion,
and collaborative activities will be used to explore weekly themes. Students will complete a ﬁnal ethnographic research
project that applies course concepts to interviews or observations conducted outside the classroom.
Regarding the use of generative AI, the course instructor will provide additional instructions separately. It will be
discussed in class at the appropriate time.
Weekly Schedule
Week 1-Introduction to culture and cultural patterns. Surface and deep culture. High and low context communication.
Week 2-Group consciousness. Individualism and collectivism.
Week 3-Uchi and soto. Social distance and in group and out group distinctions.
Week 4-Tatemae and honne. Public and private self.
Week 5-Formality and ritual. Hierarchy, giri, and omotenashi.
Week 6-Silence in communication. Ma and the values associated with silence.
Week 7-Hedataru and najimu. Relationship stages and social distance.
Week 8-Harmony and communication. Indirectness, alignment, and nonverbal communication.
Week 9-Time orientation. Punctuality and long term orientation.
Week 10-Nemawashi and decision making. Ringi seido.
Week 11-Ethnographic research workshop. Research design and interview preparation.
Week 12-Ethnographic research workshop. Data analysis and concept integration.
Week 13-Student presentations.
