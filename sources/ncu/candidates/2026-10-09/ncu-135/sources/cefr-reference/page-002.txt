 
Furthermore, with the aim of broadening students’ practical Japanese language skills, objectives are set and 
class activities are carried out by using the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR) “Can Do” 
system as a reference. Using this standardized  system, the students’ home university Japanese language 
instructors and other related individuals can share Japanese language learning progress information more easily. 
In addition, our curriculum is composed of designated classes which align with the  CEFR levels. Students in 
higher-level classes are eligible to enroll in regular undergraduate or graduate-level subjects. 
 
< Organization of Classes > 
 
Japanese Classes in the Ryukoku Japan Studies Program (JSP) are organized into four levels (JSP-Ⅱ ~ JSP-Ⅴ) 
which adhere to the CEFR framework. For each skill (Listening, Reading, Interaction, Speaking, Writing), 
learning objectives are set using “Can-Do” statements. The table below shows provides an overview of the 
“Can-Do” objectives for each class.

Can-Do Objectives 
JSP-Ⅴ 
B2.2 
The student Is able to use the language fluently, accurately, and effectively on a 
wide range of subjects. Communication is natural, using correct grammar and a 
level of politeness appropriate for the situation. Interviews are carried out 
smoothly and effectively, with questions adjusted to fit the other party and an 
ability to draw out topics of great interest to the interviewee. The student is then 
able to write an article by summarizing the contents, selecting important points 
and including supplemental facts. The student absorbs numerous points of view 
in order to gain a broader understanding of Japanese culture, one’s own culture, 
and the home culture of other international students. 
B2.1 
The student is able to converse fluently and naturally, emphasizing points which 
are important to oneself and explaining related points in detail. The student is 
assertive in firmly explaining the basis of his/her own perspective. Regarding 
his/her field of interest, is able to integrate many pieces of information from 
their source and present them as grounds for an argument. In an interview, asks 
questions relevant to the other party and can draw out various topics of interest. 
Can gather the main points and compose an article with the reader in mind. 
Absorbs numerous points of view in order to gain a broader understanding of 
Japanese culture, one’s own culture, and the home culture of other students. 
 
B1.2 
When conducting a questionnaire, the student puts together results and is able to 
provide a simple presentation using graphs and data. Can put together a short 
article and speech and can state one’s opinion. Is also able to answer detailed 
questions.  Can find the information needed from a variety of informational 
sites, etc.. Is able to conduct an easy interview while confirming information 
and asking related questions, and can combine the results and compose an 
article.
