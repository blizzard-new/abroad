Exchange students will primarily pursue their studies through the Ryukoku Japan Studies Program (JSP), which 
will be launched in April 2026. The JSP curriculum is built upon the university's guiding principle of "Magokoro" 
(sincerity, compassion, and altruism). It offers a combination of three subject groups: Japanese language study, 
international collaborative courses that involve Project-Based Learning (PBL) with domestic and other international 
students, and subjects focused on Japanese society, culture, and industry. 
 
In addition to courses offered in Japanese and elective subjects, JSP also offers many subjects taught in English. 
This allows a wide range of students to enroll, from Japanese  language beginners to advanced learners aiming to 
take the Japanese Language Proficiency Test (JLPT). This program is designed to provide significant academic and 
personal growth through field trips, cultural activities, and interaction with the local community.  Furthermore, 
students who meet certain conditions may also be permitted to enroll in courses offered by Ryukoku University’s  
undergraduate and graduate schools (All Exchange Students may register for regular subjects which are offered in 
English. Only students assigned to JSP IV・JSP V may register for regular subjects which are offered in Japanese). 
Overall, during their exchange term, students are required to register for 15 or more credits per semester.

① Ryukoku Japan Studies Program (JSP) 
This program aims to provide an opportunity for international students and local Ryukoku University students to 
learn together, deepen their understanding of different cultures and values, and develop practical communication 
skills. S tudents from diverse backgrounds collaborate in their classes through discussions, group work, and 
fieldwork. Subjects are offered in both Japanese and English. In addition, it is possible to complete the program 
entirely in English so students who have never studied Japanese are able to participate. 
If a student wishes to register for subjects conducted in Japanese, they must be assessed as having the necessary 
level of Japanese proficiency (based on the results of the Placement Test). If the student’s Japanese proficiency is 
not sufficient, they will take classes conducted in English.
