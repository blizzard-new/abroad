Aschaffenburg University of Applied Sciences 
(Aschaffenburg UAS) is one of Germany’s leading 
young universities. Since becoming a teaching 
institution in 1995, Aschaffenburg UAS has 
offered forward-looking programmes geared 
towards putting the latest research into practice. 
Today, more than 180 professors and lecturers 
teach approximately 3,750 students. 
Top-ranked programmes
Due to its achievements in teaching and research 
as well as the international outlook of its 
programmes of study, Aschaffenburg UAS has 
been rated among Germany’s leading institutions 
in various rankings.
Meaningful interdisciplinary collaboration
Due to its focused yet interconnected three-
faculty organisational structure, students have 
easy access to opportunities for interdisciplinary 
collaboration. Business and law students have 
a chance to gain technology expertise, for 
instance, while engineers can brush up on their 
management skills.

Informal and welcoming atmosphere
The compact campus is easily walkable, and the 
small size creates a strong community feeling. 
On a typical day on campus, you might bump 
into friends on your way to class, eat lunch with a 
professor in the dining hall, and carry out research 
with classmates in a high-tech laboratory – all 
within a five- or ten-minute walk. 
Excellence in teaching and personal support
World-class teaching is one of Aschaffenburg 
UAS’s most important goals, and its teachers 
have extensive resources to ensure that you 
maximise your learning. Small class sizes mean 
that your professors and lecturers are available to 
you for extra guidance or support, both during and 
outside of seminars. 
Modern infrastructure, high-tech facilities
Classrooms, lecture halls, offices, and laboratories 
are housed in historical buildings that have 
been completely renovated in order to provide a 
modern, high-tech study environment. Laboratory 
and workshop facilities are cutting-edge, offering 
you access to the latest equipment in your chosen 
field, and classrooms and study spaces are 
equipped with modern computing technology. 
Friendly town in a booming business region
Aschaffenburg lies in the heart of the Frankfurt-
Rhine-Main business region – Germany’s 
industrial and business core. We cultivate 
extensive partnerships with regional and global 
companies, offering students ample opportunities 
to put theory into practice, find part-time jobs, 
internships, and post-graduation employment. 
At the same time, Aschaffenburg is small and 
friendly, with lovely architecture and historical 
charm. Extensive public transportation provides 
easy access to the rest of the region and 
Germany, giving students the best of both worlds.

Academic information and application
In general, German higher education institutions 
are classified as either universities or universities 
of applied sciences. As a university of applied 
sciences, Aschaffenburg UAS focuses more on 
gaining skills and knowledge which can be directly 
applied in professional life. Universities of applied 
sciences are more industry-oriented than their 
counterparts and professors must have practical 
work experience outside of the higher education 
sector. Both types of institutions can issue 
bachelor’s and master’s degrees.
Course offer
The English-taught courses cover a wide 
range of subjects. Depending on your German 
language proficiency, you may also choose from 
a wide variety of courses taught in German. At 
Aschaffenburg UAS you can study business 
administration, real estate management, 
computing, renewable energies, mechatronics, 
sales, and material sciences, to name a few.
 www.th-ab.de/course-offer

German as a Foreign Language
Learning German can be very useful to connect 
with the locals. Aschaffenburg UAS offers German 
language courses for all skill levels – whether 
you are starting out as a beginner or at a more 
advanced level. In addition to the courses offered 
during the semester, Aschaffenburg UAS offers 
four-week intensive German courses in August.
 www.th-ab.de/learngerman
Credit system and grading
A full-time workload is 30 ECTS credits per 
semester. Grades are awarded from 1,0 (highest) 
to 5,0 (lowest) with 4,0 as the minimum passing 
grade. Some courses are evaluated on a pass / 
fail grading system.
Erasmus+ and exchange students will receive 
credits for all successfully completed courses and 
all obtained credits will be listed on a transcript 
of records. Students can download the transcript 
of records themselves, and it will also be sent 
directly to the home institution.

Fee
No fees will be charged by Aschaffenburg UAS, 
but all students have to pay the semester fee. In 
Germany, the Studentenwerk maintains a range 
of services such as the campus dining hall, 
the halls of residence, or counselling services. 
According to the Bavarian Higher Education Act, 
all students are to pay a small service fee for 
the administration of these services – currently 
70 EUR (as of March 2025).
Nomination and application 
Exchange students are nominated by their home 
university before they apply at Aschaffenburg UAS 
using an online application form. 
 www.th-ab.de/apply-online

Summer semester: 20 November / 1 December

Housing
The Studierendenwerk runs two halls of residence 
on and near the campus, renting out furnished 
single rooms in shared apartments as well 
as furnished single apartments. Wheelchair 
accessible apartments are available. Please 
apply by 15 June (winter semester) / 15 January 
(summer semester). For a small fee and upon 
request, the Studierendenwerk also rents 
household packages that include bedding. 
 www.studentenwerk-wuerzburg.de/en/
A room in the halls of residence is not 
guaranteed. This is why we recommend that 
you look for private accommodation as well. 
In Germany, many students live in flat shares 
(Wohngemeinschaft / WG). Each tenant has their 
own room but the kitchen and bathroom are 
shared. The cost of electricity, water, internet and 
telephone is also shared. For a furnished room in 
a flat share, you can expect to pay EUR 500–700.

to let:

Student life 
Joining university sports (Hochschulsport) is 
a great way to make friends and meet local 
students while studying with us. The programme 
usually includes a mix of exercise classes and 
current trend sports, all taught by experienced 
trainers. Everyone is welcome to participate, 
from beginners to advanced athletes. In general, 
participation is free of charge and possible 
without prior registration.
There are also various student organisations on 
campus. The Studentenvertretung is the student 
representation at Aschaffenburg UAS. They 
meet biweekly and discuss issues of student 
welfare as well as organise events and activities. 
As an exchange student you have the same 
opportunities as our degree-seeking students to 
join the Studentenvertretung or any of our other 
student organisations, such as Rolling Students, 
Economics, or Students meet Real Estate e. V.

The Federal Republic of Germany is located 
directly in the heart of Europe and extends from 
the Baltic Sea in the north to the Alps in the south. 
It is not big in size but offers one of the highest 
standards of living in the world as well as diverse 
art and culture, and it is well-known for its high-
quality products. With its 84 million inhabitants 
it has the largest population of all EU member 
states.
Living and studying in Germany
Germany is often seen as a byword for stability. 
It is a parliamentary democracy and its citizens 
trust in fair elections and the rule of law – it is 
a safe country where you can travel freely and 
feel secure. A German proverb claims that ‘five 
minutes ahead of time is the German punctuality’. 
Germans are undoubtedly dependent and reliable. 
At the same time, German society is diverse, 
colourful and cosmopolitan. To most Germans, 
equality is an important value – living free from 
worry and with equal opportunities regardless of 
gender, race, sexual orientation, or religion.
The country is also considered an economic 
giant. Its machinery and electrical equipment 
are known for their high quality and durability 
and the country is one of the largest automobile 
producers in the world. While Germany can be 
proud of the richness of its economy, perhaps 
even more notable is its richness of human life – 
people have opportunities and choices. Germany 
has consistently been ranked among the best 
countries to live in by the Human Development 
Report.
 www.deutschland.de/en

Practicalities
Visa
EU and EFTA nationals do not require a visa to 
enter Germany. If you are not an EU national, 
please contact the German Embassy or German 
Consulate in your country of residence to check 
visa regulations as well as residence permit 
regulations prior to travelling to Germany. Do not 
enter with a tourist visa. 
 www.auswaertiges-amt.de/en
Arrival 
Aschaffenburg is located around 40 km from 
Frankfurt, where a major international airport 
is located, making it easy to reach the area by 
plane. From “FRA Frankfurt Airport” you can take 
a train to “Aschaffenburg Hbf”. There are also two 
smaller train stations in town: Aschaffenburg Süd  
and Aschaffenburg Hochschule, which is located 
next to Aschaffenburg UAS.
 www.bahn.com/en

Registration in town
All students have to register their place of 
residence within two weeks of their arrival at 
the town hall’s Residents’ Registration Office 
(Einwohnermeldeamt). After the registration 
you will receive a confirmation called 
Meldebescheinigung that is often required to open 
a German bank account. 
Please bring:
    your passport / ID card,
    your certificate of enrolment,
    your rental contract and documents 
(Wohnungsgeberbescheinigung).
Insurance
It is mandatory as a student to have health 
insurance cover for your entire stay in Germany. 
EU and EFTA nationals are entitled to medical 
treatment with their European Health Insurance 
Card. Students from outside EU/EFTA countries 
should sign up for statutory or private health 
insurance. We recommend taking out a policy 
with a German statutory health insurance 
company. These institutions offer special prices 
for students — approx. 130 EUR / month for 
health and long-term care insurance.
In addition, we recommend taking out personal 
liability insurance (Haftpflichtversicherung). 
Although it is not compulsory, it covers unintentional 
damage to a third party or a third party’s property.

Buddy programme
As an international student, you will be matched 
with a local “buddy” who will help you get settled 
in Aschaffenburg. Your buddy is a full-time 
student at Aschaffenburg UAS and will meet you 
upon arrival, show you around and help you with 
practical matters, such as registration or opening 
a bank account. During the semester, the buddies 
will organise social events and trips, and answer 
social questions throughout your stay.
Welcome Weeks
Prior to the start of the semester, the International 
Office organises two (compulsory) Welcome 
Weeks. You will have the chance to learn some 
German, clear all administrative milestones, and 
participate in various events and activities. This is 
a great opportunity to settle in, meet new people 
and make some friends.
Cost of living
Prices in Germany are in line with the EU average. 
The German government estimates the average 
expenditure for students to be approx. EUR 934 
a month, which includes accommodation, food, 
study materials, leisure, and transport. There are 
many generous grant and scholarship schemes 
that can help to cover your cost of living during 
your stay. 
 www.daad.de/en 
Licence fee
Every household in Germany is required to pay 
a licence fee (Rundfunkbeitrag) of 18.36 EUR /
month for public service broadcasting. The fee is 
subject to the principle of solidarity. It is relatively 
small because everyone pays regardless of   
actual media use. 
 www.rundfunkbeitrag.de/welcome
