Important Notice
The Housing Advice Service does not own or manage any rooms or apartments, nor do we function as an agency that connects housing providers with students. Our role is to offer information and guidance on navigating the Aachen housing market and to answer any questions you may have about accommodation. Ultimately, the responsibility for securing a place to live based on our recommendations lies with the students themselves.
Discover essential information, helpful hints, and practical tips on securing accommodation in Aachen by watching our video Looking for Accommodation in Aachen – The Basics.
If you’re preparing to study or conduct research in Aachen, it is essential to begin your housing search as early as possible. RWTH Aachen University does not provide student dormitories, and while there are guest houses available for doctoral candidates and visiting researchers, space is very limited. To assist you, our Housing Advice Service for International Students and the Welcome Center for International Researchers are here to guide you in finding suitable accommodations.
Each winter semester, RWTH welcomes over 9,000 new students, as well as hundreds of new employees and visiting researchers. With such high demand, finding affordable housing in Aachen can be challenging, particularly as the semester approaches.
We strongly recommend starting your housing search 2-3 months before your planned arrival. This proactive approach will increase your chances of securing suitable living arrangements.
RWTH offers a small number of
guesthouses
located directly on campus for overnight stays by doctoral candidates and visiting researchers. However, these options are in high demand, and early reservations – ideally several months in advance – are necessary. Bookings must be arranged through your host institute. Be sure to contact your institute representative well in advance to check availability.
For additional options, various private listings for temporary and furnished apartments in Aachen are available on the
Wohnen auf Zeit
web portal. These listings can be a practical solution for short-term or flexible housing needs.

Ways to find accommodation - FAQ
Does RWTH own student dormitories/residence halls?
No, RWTH does not own, rent, or manage any student residence halls or other campus housing. However,
Studierendenwerk Aachen
and other private dorms offer affordable housing options for students in and around Aachen.
Where can I find an overview of housing options in Aachen?
You can refer to our
Housing Guide
, which provides a comprehensive overview of short-, medium-, and long-term housing options in Aachen and its surrounding areas. The guide includes links to websites, approximate pricing, and details on application or booking procedures.
Am I eligible to apply for one of the “quota rooms” reserved by the International Office in collaboration with Studierendenwerk Aachen?
Generally, degree-seeking students are not eligible for quota rooms.
However, exchange, or program students staying at RWTH for one or two semesters may qualify. To apply for a quota room, you must first submit an application with
Incoming Student Services
within the appropriate deadlines. If eligible, the Housing Advice Service will contact you with access to the application portal and your applicant number.
Please note that the International Office oversees the application process. However, Studierendenwerk, as the dormitory owner, is responsible for room offers, allocation, and all related procedures, including lease contracts, rent payments, and deposit refunds.
Can RWTH or the International Office help me get a dorm room faster?
Unfortunately, no. The Studierendenwerk Aachen and other private dormitories operate independently of RWTH Aachen University. The University and the International Office have no influence over the allocation process or waiting lists.
I would like to live in the Netherlands or in Belgium. Is that possible?
Students with German or EU citizenship are eligible to reside in the Netherlands or in Belgium. Non-EU students must apply for a residence permit to study in Germany, which requires having a registered address within the country. As a result, non-EU students cannot reside in the Netherlands or in Belgium.
What is a housing entitlement certificate – or Wohnberechtigungsschein – and am I eligible for one?
Some publicly funded, lower-priced apartments have specific tenancy requirements, including the need for a housing entitlement certificate (Wohnberechtigungsschein). Both German and international students can apply for this certificate. Students with limited income may be eligible for the Wohnberechtigungsschein. To check your eligibility, consult the
WBS Rechner
or the
City of Aachen housing office webpages
. Please note that both websites are available only in German.
Renting a room or apartment - FAQ
I have questions about my rental agreement. Whom can I contact?
If you have questions about your rental agreement or are experiencing issues with your landlord or landlady, you can reach out to the
legal advice team
at the AStA Students’ Committee. This team offers professional legal advice, helping you understand your rights and obligations as a tenant.
Can I pay the rent late?
The first month’s rent and deposit are typically required before you move in, after both parties sign the lease agreement. Most contracts include a clause specifying the latest possible payment date (e.g., the third of the month). If you anticipate a delay in payment, it’s important to notify your dorm administration, landlord, or landlady as soon as possible. Be aware that late payments may incur additional fees.
How do I do I register my address with the city authorities (Wohnsitzanmeldung)?
After moving into your new residence, make sure to get the form called
Wohnungsgeberbestätigung
signed by your landlord, landlady, or dorm administration. Then, schedule an appointment at the Citizens’ Office in Aachen using the city’s online
appointment tool
. This must be done within two weeks of your move-in date. Bring the signed Wohnungsgeberbestätigung form and your passport or ID to the appointment. You can find additional information about the registration process in German at
Serviceportal der Stadt Aachen
.
What should I do if my bank account is frozen and I can’t transfer the rent or deposit?
If your bank account is temporarily frozen, you may ask a relative or friend to assist with the initial payment. Once you’ve registered your address in Aachen, you’ll be able to unblock or open a local bank account using your address registration confirmation.
I would like to rent out or sublet an apartment. Whom should I contact?
If you’re looking to rent out or sublet your apartment or house, please visit our dedicated
web pages for landladies and landlords
. These pages provide detailed guidance on the process.
I’m living in a Studierendenwerk residence hall. What do I need to know about moving out?
If you’re living in an Studierendenwerk residence hall, note the following guidelines:
Even though your rental agreement runs until the last day of the month, move-outs are only allowed on working days (Monday to Friday).
You must schedule an official room inspection with the janitor in advance for key handover.
Approximately 6 to 8 weeks before your contract ends, a “pre-inspection” will be conducted to identify any larger repairs that may need to be addressed.
Failing to follow the proper move-out procedures or refusing the “pre-inspection” can result in a fee being deducted from your deposit. For more details, refer to the
Studierendenwerk Questions and Answers page
.
When can I get my deposit back?
For residents in Studierendenwerk residence halls, deposit refunds typically take 12 to 14 weeks after move-out. If the process takes longer, contact
Studierendenwerk
directly.
If you rented on the private market, it may take up to six months to receive your deposit. In either case, be sure to keep a copy of your move-out protocol until your deposit is fully reimbursed.
Beware of scams - FAQ
How can I identify an online housing scam?
Scammers often target international students using specific tricks. Many of these methods are summarized on resources like
iamexpat.de
. In general, use common sense and avoid sharing sensitive documents or transferring money without absolute proof that the accommodation offer and the person you are corresponding with are legitimate.
Where are scammers most active?
Scammers frequently operate on social media platforms like Facebook and popular housing portals such as WG-Gesucht and Ebay Kleinanzeigen. While it’s uncommon, they may contact you directly through these platforms.
Additionally, some scammers use temporary housing websites like Booking.com and Airbnb to post fake listings. Be cautious of listings that require payments through external transfers rather than the platform’s secure payment system, as this is often a sign of fraud.
The person offering the room or apartment says they are outside Germany and wants me to transfer a security deposit. Is this safe?
This scenario is a common scam tactic. The alleged owner might claim they are abroad and unable to conduct an in-person viewing. They may offer to send the apartment key by mail or provide it through an intermediary but request an advance deposit to “secure” the property. Once you make the payment, the scammer will disappear, leaving you without a key or further communication. In some cases, they might even demand additional money, claiming the initial transfer didn’t go through.
Should I reserve and pay for accommodation without seeing it in person?
If you are unable to visit a property in person before renting – such as when applying for student housing from abroad – it is critical to verify the legitimacy of the listing. For institutional accommodations, look for official websites or verified credentials, such as recognizable logos or company stamps.
For private market rentals, exercise extra caution. If you’ve communicated with a landlord or lessor but haven’t had the opportunity to view the property firsthand, ensure you thoroughly verify the authenticity of the listing before making any payments or sharing sensitive information. Avoid relying solely on photographs or online correspondence via email or messaging apps like WhatsApp. Whenever possible, request a video tour or seek assistance from trusted advisors or local housing support services.
What should I do if I suspect a scam or realize I’ve been scammed?
Ignore the scammer: Do not respond to their demands. Do not transfer money under any circumstances. Avoid cashing checks or forwarding letters/packages. If you have already transferred money, attempt to reverse the payment immediately if possible.
Document everything: Save all correspondence, including emails, chat messages, and transaction receipts, as evidence. Keep copies of bank transfer confirmations or any other related documents.
Seek support: Report the incident to your local police station. They will guide you on how to secure evidence and file a formal report. Please also inform RWTH’s Housing Advice Service about the incident. While prosecuting scammers can be challenging, especially if they operate from abroad, reporting these cases can help prevent others from falling victim to the same schemes.
