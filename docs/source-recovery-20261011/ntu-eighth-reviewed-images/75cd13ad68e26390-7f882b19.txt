Keio International Program(KIP)
The Keio International Program (KIP) allows students to
study about Japan and East/Southeast Asia in English
and
take Japanese language courses
as well.
This comprehensive program is intended to be open to
undergraduate
and
master's graduate students
.
As Keio local students can also take the International Center courses, exchange students will have opportunities to study alongside Keio students.
Once selecting this program, students are not allowed to change it to other ones.
Program Requirements
KIP students must register for
a minimum of 7 courses per semester
.
We have no requirements on the number of
credits
.
There is no Japanese proficiency required
to apply for the program.
Program Outline
Students enrolled in the Keio International Program (KIP) can take courses from any combination of
International Center Courses
,
Keio Japanese Courses(KJC)
which are Japanese language courses, and
Courses offered by Faculties/ Graduate Schools
(Japanese or English taught).
You will be asked to fill out a Study Proposal when you apply, but official registration will take place after you arrive in Japan.
Please note that there is a possibility that you may not be able to take the courses you wish to take due to course restrictions, course capacity limits, etc.
If you wish to apply, please plan your study with flexibility.

International Center Courses

courses focusing on Japan and East/Southeast Asia as a central theme.

Keio Japanese Courses (KJC) (Japanese language courses for non-JLP students)
Keio Japanese Courses are open to KIP students ranging from elementary to advanced level, in which students can effectively learn the Japanese language in a short period of time and balance their studies in Japanese language with other courses.
Language of Instruction: Japanese
Maximum number of courses taken: Up to 4 courses per semester
For more details, please visit
here

Courses offered by Undergraduate Faculties/ Graduate Schools
Students may also take Courses offered by Faculties/ Graduate Schools, which are mostly taught in Japanese.
Limited number of courses are available for exchange students, and priority is given to degree-seeking students.
