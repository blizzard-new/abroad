How to Choose a Course
Preliminary course catalog
In the preliminary course catalog, you’ll find a list of courses that may be
available during upcoming semesters.
It also includes information on courses offered in previous terms.
You can filter the course list by language of instruction (English or Spanish),
campus, school, and department. If you need more detailed information
about a specific course, you can view its syllabus.
Understanding the courses
Tec de Monterrey courses’ code contains bits of information, such as the course level and the area
of study. This information is particularly relevant for your learning agreement and course selection.
Here's an example of how to read a course code:
MT 1 001 B Marketing Insight Develop Strategies
Course nameCourse code
The letters (at the beginning of the code) specify to which area of knowledge the course
belongs (For example: MT is for Marketing).
The numbers* indicate the semester in which our TEC students register the course, for example:
Code that contains 1000 are first level courses offered during the first semesters of a
bachelor (Semesters 1 to 3).
Code that contains 2000 are intermediate or advanced leve courses offered during the
middle of a bachelor (Semesters 3 to 6).
Code that contains 3000 are advanced leve courses offered during the last semester of a
bachelor (Semesters 6 to 8).
The letter at the end of some courses stands for a block or a concentration (B for blocks, C for
Concentrations).
*Note. A Tec de Monterrey bachelor is usually 8 to 9 semesters long. 10

Classes and Schedule
Usually Tec de Monterrey semesters are composed by courses, blocks and Tec Weeks, and is
divided into 3 cycles that last 5 weeks each.
Courses are like a traditional course, and that can last 5, 10, or 15 weeks depending on the
subject and credits.
Blocks are courses that contain challenges connected to reality and theoretical modules. You
can only participate in one block per period. 
TEC Weeks are weeks between cycles where you can participate in an activity or experience
designed to develop different skills. Tec Weeks are not mandatory for international students.
In the case of a Concentration, this specialization is equivalent to several blocks and courses that
runs through the whole semester.

One of Tec de Monterrey main concerns is the health and safety of all our
community; therefore, it is MANDATORY for all international students to be covered
by an international health insurance plan.
