Okinawa Prefecture, the southernmost prefecture of Japan, comprises approximately 691 islands. The prefecture's 
main island, the island of Okinawa, has a population of about 1.4 million. Formerly known as the Ryukyu Kingdom, 
Okinawa has a history of intimate cultural exchange with China, Oceania, and countries in Southeast Asia, leading to 
the creation of a distinctive culture rich with unique arts, rituals and cuisine. 
Today, Okinawa continues to take advantage of its historical and geographical position to encourage economic, 
cultural, and educational interaction in Asia and the Pacific region, fulfilling an important role in international ex-
change as the "southern gate to Japan." 
As the only subtropical region in Japan, Okinawa is blessed with a warm climate throughout the year and experienc-
es relatively low seasonal and day/night temperature variation. Okinawa is known both within and outside of Japan 
as a place with a brilliant range of flora and fauna, emerald-blue seas, white beaches and amazing coral reefs. It is 
home to many endemic plants and animals and is recognized as an international tourist destination, with increasing 
numbers of visitors each year.
Okinawa Prefecture University of the Ryukyus
The University of the Ryukyus was founded after the end of World 
War II in May 1950, when Okinawa was under American military 
administration. The University of the Ryukyus gained its present 
status as a national university when Okinawa was returned to Ja -
pan in May 1972. 
Currently, the University composes  7 faculties and 8 graduate 
schools and has more than 8,000 students. The University of the 
Ryukyus includes the University Library, the University Hospital, 
the Tropical Biosphere Research Center, the Global Education Cen-
ter, the Lifelong Education and Research Center, the University Ed-
ucation Center, the Subtropical Fields Science Center, and a Uni -
versity-affiliated junior high school and elementary school.
Faculties and Graduate Schools
The Global Education Center provides courses for studying Japa -
nese, as well as the history and culture of Japan and Okinawa. The 
Global Education Center also conducts career counseling for inter-
national students seeking employment opportunities in Japan. 
Most study abroad students belong to the Global Education Cen-
ter. Please refer to page 13 for more information on the different 
courses offered at the Center and the University.

02. Ryukyus International Student Exchange Program
Objective
The Ryukyus International Student Exchange (RISE) Program is an arrangement made between the University and our 
partner universities abroad that enables students to experience student life at our campus while conducting research 
and taking classes related to students’ majors. 
This program provides students with a deeper understanding of both Okinawan and Japanese culture and customs. 
It also gives students the opportunity to return to Japan in the future to pursue graduate studies or research.
Courses Offered
(1)  Japan-Okinawa Studies Course
(2)  Glocal Leadership Course
(3)  Japanese Language Teacher Training Course
(4)  Major Studies Course 
Admission and Period of Stay
1. April Admission
(1) Apr. - Aug. (Spring semester only; approximately half a year)
(2) Apr. - Feb. (Spring & Fall semesters; approximately one year)
2. October Admission
(1) Oct.- Feb. (Fall semester only; approximately half a year)
(2) Oct. - Aug. (Fall & Spring semesters; approximately one year)
Admission Requirements
Requirements for all students applying for the 
RISE Program are as follows:
1. The student's institution must be affiliated with 
the University of the Ryukyus or be a partner of 
the UMAP or ISEP.
2. The student must either be enrolled as a full-
time undergraduate student who has completed 
his/her first year of study at his/her home institu-
tion or be matriculated as a graduate student. Ju -
nior college students who apply must be in their 
final year of study. 
3. The students must be proficient in Japanese at 
the level required by each given course. 
4. The students must have a non-Japanese pass -
port.
Students must be enrolled in their home univer-
sities and should not be expected to graduate 
during the period of exchange. 
Enrollment Status
Once accepted, students will be given the status of “Spe -
cial Auditor”, while graduate school students in the Major 
Studies Course will have the status of “Special Research 
Student” at the University.
Tuition and Fees
Examination fees, admission fee and tuition will not be 
charged to students whose institutions are covered by a Stu-
dent Exchange Agreement that stipulates a mutual exemp-
tion of tuition fees.
Academic Assessment
(1) Our academic grading system takes the form of five letter grades.

(2) Official transcripts of completed courses and credits earned will be issued by the administrative authorities of the
     department to which the student belongs.  
(3) Decisions on whether acquired credits may be counted towards students’ graduation requirements shall rest with
    their respective home universities. 
Glocal Festival
This event aims to deepen cultural exchange between international and Japanese students, as well as the local 
community through various activities such as games, international cuisine and stage performances. International 
students are invited to introduce their countries and cultures to guests at this festival.

03. Course Description—Japan-Okinawa Studies Course
The Japan-Okinawa Studies Course is for students who wish to 
learn the history and culture of Japan and Okinawa while develop-
ing their Japanese language skills at the same time. 
Students can learn and develop cross-cultural communication 
skills by interacting with exchange students from all over the 
world. Japanese classes are available from beginner to advanced 
levels. There are general Japanese classes that develop four skills 
— reading, listening, speaking and writing. Students can learn 
Japanese at a pace that best suits their needs, even if they have 
never learnt the language before. 
History and culture classes are offered in both Japanese and En-
glish, and with frequent opportunities for fieldwork. Students can 
also take classes attended primarily by Japanese students that are 
aligned to their majors and/or areas of interests.

               Glocal Engagement Seminar
In this course, international students, Japanese stu-
dents, and local citizens collaborate on regional de-
velopment and share their findings and outcomes 
through the “Glocal Festival”. 
In the festival, participants create presentations and 
exhibitions of diverse cultural specialties that include 
regional languages, traditional practices, religions, so-
cial issues, food, music, performances, games etc. Dis-
cussions and presentations are managed “champ-lin-
gually”, mixing use of diverse languages including 
Japanese and English, remaining true to the spirit of 
“leaving no one behind”. Seminar activities are aimed 
at nurturing budding glocal leadership.

Major Studies Course - Japanese Language and Culture Studies Course
 (Provided by the Faculty of Global and Regional Studies, Global Languages and Cultures Program,  Japanese Culture Course)
The Global Languages and Cultures Program of the Faculty of Global and Regional Studies provides a unique 
course for students who are majoring in Japanese language and culture at their home institution.
Who will Benefit
Undergraduate or graduate school students ma-
joring in Japanese language or Japanese culture 
at their home universities, who intend to acquire 
advanced knowledge in Japanese language and 
Japanese society and social structures. 
Learning Goals
(1)Develop independent thinking and learning 
not only in the classroom but also through ob -
servational and hands-on learning.
(2)Acquire advanced Japanese language skills 
through in-depth study of literature, linguistics, 
social conditions, current Japanese affairs, as well 
as the history and background of Japanese and 
Okinawan culture within courses designed for 
students majoring in Japanese.
Language Requirements
JLPT N2 (equivalent or above) is required. 
This course is designed for students with an advanced level of Japanese (C1 or C2 level). 
If your main purpose is to improve your Japanese language skills, please apply for the
 "Japan and Okinawa Study Course."
The Major Studies Course is provided to students who wish delve 
into deeper learning about their field of specialization.
Undergraduate exchange students attend lectures related to their 
majors, while graduate students conduct research and attend 
topic-centered study groups known widely in Japanese universi -
ties as “seminars”. By working together on research with Japanese 
students of the same field, students will be able to develop new

06. Campus Life — Living Expenses/ Other Expenses
Living Expenses
The costs of living in Okinawa are relatively low compared to 
other cities in Japan. However, students require approximately 
80,000 yen a month to cover personal expenses. Students are 
advised to make adequate preparations for living arrange -
ments in the case that they do not qualify for a scholarship. 
Due to the demands of the program schedule, students are 
also advised not to take on part-time work. As students will not 
be able to open a bank account immediately after their arrival 
in Okinawa, it is necessary for them to have sufficient cash to 
pay for two to three months' worth of living expenses, or have 
a credit card that can be used overseas.
National Health Insurance
The National Health Insurance program provides fi-
nancial assistance in the case of illness or injury. The 
annual insurance premium is approximately 16,000 
yen and must be paid in full. Up to 70% of medical 
fees will be covered by the insurance program when 
insurance cards are presented at the time of treat -
ment. The remaining 30% of medical costs will be 
borne by the student. All exchange students are re-
quired to join the National Health Insurance program 
immediately after arriving in Japan.
Accident Insurance
The University recommends all exchange students to enroll in 
accident insurance to protect and prepare themselves against 
accidents. Accident insurance consists of two components: (1) 
Personal Accident Insurance for Students Pursuing Education 
and Research (PAS) (valid for unexpected accidents that occur 
when students are engaged in regular curricular activities, 
school events, extracurricular activities and when students are 
on school premises or commuting to and from such activities) 
and, (2) Liability Insurance Coupled With PAS (students enrolled 
in this insurance scheme are entitled to seek damages for acci-
dents caused to third persons such as bodily injuries and de -
struction of personal property when students engaged in regu-
lar curricular activities, school events, extracurricular activities 
and when students are on school premises or commuting to 
and from such activities). The total annual insurance premium is 
approximately 1,340 yen.
