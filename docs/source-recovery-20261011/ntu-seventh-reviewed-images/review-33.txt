European Credit Transfer System (ECTS) and SWS (“Semesterwochenstunden”)
The number of ECTS (credits) depends on the workload of a course, based on factors such as the number of teaching hours (SWS = "Semesterwochenstunden" / "SWS", which means semester hours per week), number and length of written or oral exams and preparatory and self-study. 1 ECTS = 30 hours of work, 1 hour of work = 45 minutes, 1 SWS = usually 15 attendance hours of 45 minutes each.
Please note that the number of credit points per course is fixed. Hence you cannot obtain less credit points for only partial fulfillment of the course requirements. It is either full credit points or none at all.
Exams and papers are marked with grades from 1 to 5, the best grade being 1, and 5 indicating failure. In order to get a more differentiated evaluation, each grade can be raised and lowered by 0.3. The grades can be transferred into ECTS grades according to the following table:

Student housing for exchange and visiting students
A limited number of rooms in student housing are available for exchange students to apply for. The International Office also supports with finding a private room.
Applying for student housing
The
Munich Student Union
provides a very limited number of places for exchange students in
student housing
. This is referred to as the
service package
. The International Office is responsible for allocating student housing. It is not possible to apply directly to the Student Union.
Please note that applications received after the
application deadline
will not be considered for student housing.
Rooms are rented from September to the end of February (winter semester), as well as March to the end of July (summer semester); usually with a maximum rental period of one semester. The average rent is between 400 € and 600 € per month (depending on the location / student dorm).
If you are a Bachelor or Master student, and going to study at LMU as an
Erasmus+, UK, SEMP or LMUexchange student
, you can apply for a room in student housing directly in your
online application
(by ticking the corresponding box).
If you are studying at LMU for one or two semesters as part of a
double degree
or
Erasmus Mundus
program, you can ask your coordinator about student housing options.
If you are a PhD student, or do not belong to the group of above named exchange programs, or do not wish to apply for a place in student housing, please look for accommodation yourself as early as possible. We have prepared useful information about finding private accommodation in Munich below.
Please note that rooms can only be guaranteed for
approximately half of the students
! Due to the challenging housing market in Munich, please start looking for accommodation as soon as possible, while simultaneously stating in your online application that you are applying for student housing. The International Office will support you with additional resources to help you find private accommodation. We recommend to make use of this support to avoid getting scammed.
Receiving a student housing offer
If you are offered a room in student housing, the Munich Student Union will send you an email with more detailed information about the accommodation as well as a document called an
Annahmeerklärung
(declaration of acceptance). Please sign this document, return it to the Munich Student Union and transfer three months’ rent plus the deposit. You can find the exact amount in your acceptance letter. The room is reserved for you once the Munich Student Union has received the signed document. Please note that most of the rooms will be given for one semester only for winter term, however, for summer term only for one semester without exception.
Useful information for finding private accommodation
Unfortunately, we cannot guarantee you a room. If we are unable to provide you with student housing from the Munich Student Union, the International Office can assist you in finding a
private room (PDF, 100 KB)
.
Please send a completed
application form for private accommodation (PDF, 148 KB)
to
housing-incoming@lmu.de
.
The
Munich Student Union
or the
Jugendinformationszentrum (Youth Information Center)
also have resources that can help you find a place to stay.
In the
overview information on cases of fraud (PDF, 143 KB)
you can find further information and how you can best recognize those cases.

Green Travel
We would like to encourage you to consider low-emission means of transportation such as bus, train, bicycle or carpooling within Europe. In doing so, you will be making a significant contribution to environmental protection. Of course, travel times are sometimes significantly longer than when traveling by air. On the other hand, you have the opportunity to organize your trip in stages and thus get to know the places between LMU and your home university. This also gives you the chance to arrive in Munich in a relaxed and unhurried manner.
Please find further information on the website
Erasmus by Train
.
The Interrail ticket is a good way to travel sustainably within Europe. For students who are sponsored by the Erasmus program, it also offers a special discount - the normal validity period of one month is extended to six months. This means that if you spend a semester in Munich, you can travel there and back with just one ticket!
Further information can be found on the
Interrail website
.
LMU has launched a partnership with Go2RAIL – Travel Together, a non-profit start-up helping students explore Europe by train! Go2RAIL offers a great way for students to make their travel easier and safer; with features like meeting other students on their route, planning trips together, making journeys safer—especially on night trains—and traveling more environmentally friendly.
Learn more & join the movement on the

It is worth opening a German or European bank account (in euros) even for a short stay of one or two semesters. This saves you numerous transaction fees. Many banks have a branch network where you can open a checking account in person. There are also direct banks where opening an account is only possible online.

Certificate of registration with LMU or Letter of admission from LMU

Basic fee
Each semester all students must pay the
basic fee
as part of their registration or continuation of registration, even in the case of a leave-of-absence semester. There are
no
exceptions.
The basic fee is due at the time of registration or
continuation of registration
. According to the
official notifications
, they must be paid in one lump sum by the specified
deadlines
. If the fee due is not paid on time by the deadlines, the student‘s registration will be cancelled or the continuation of registration will not be completed and the student will be exmatriculated.
The
fee amount
for the basic fee is determined by the statutes of the Studierendenwerk München Oberbayern (Munich Student Union). The University both charges these fees for and then tranfers them to the Studierendenwerk, in accordance with Art. 121 section 5 sentence 1 of the Bayerische Hochschulinnovationsgesetz. For further information regarding the basic fee and its purpose, please refer to the websites of the
Studierendenwerk München Oberbayern
.
Additional fee for the semester ticket
According to an update provided by the Studierendenwerk München Oberbayern, the agreement on the semester ticket will be suspended as of the Winter Semester 2023/24. Thus, as of the Winter Semester 2023/24 the required additional fee for the semester ticket will be discontinued until further notice. As part of the continuation of registration only the basic fee must be paid at present.
Please note that as of the Winter Semester 2023/24 your student ID card (LMUcard) can no longer be used as a valid MVV ticket, not even during the off hours (Mon to Fri, 6 pm until 6 am, nor on weekends)! For further information on finding alternatives to the semester ticket, please refer to
website of the Studierendenwerk
at as well as to those of the transport authorities.
