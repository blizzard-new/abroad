Visa and Entry
If you would like to come to TU Berlin as an international mobile researcher or student, you may require a visa to enter Germany. This will depend on your nationality. This page provides general information for students and visiting scholars on applying for visas. If you have any questions about the application process, our team will be happy to help. It is also advisable to contact the German diplomatic mission or consulate in your home country directly if you have questions about applying for a visa.
Students from third countries usually require a visa to enter Germany. Visas must be applied for from the German embassy or consulate in the student's own country.
Mostly,
a visa for studying
is issued, which usually is only valid for three months. You then have to apply for a
residence permit for the purpose of full-time studies
before the visa expires. As such, it makes more sense to obtain a visa for the entire duration of your stay, if possible.
You can find an overview of the countries whose citizens can
enter without a visa
and then apply for
a residence permit for the purpose of studies after arriving in Germany on the
website of the Berlin Immigration Office
.
When deciding on
health insurance
, please keep in mind to the
information provided by the
Berlin Immigration Office
.
EU citizens and citizens of EEA states only need to register with the
Citizens' Registration Office (Bürgeramt)
.
Please note: It is not possible to convert a tourist visa to a student visa after entering the Federal Republic of Germany.
Before entering Germany, apply for the visa you are eligible for at the German diplomatic mission (consulate) in your home country.
Allow sufficient time for your application. In some countries, it can take up to several months or longer to obtain a visa.
If possible, try to get a visa covering the entire duration of your studies at TU Berlin. This will save you having to apply for a student residence permit in Berlin. All you have to do then is register in a
Citizens' Registration Office (Bürgeramt)
.

Types of visa
You can apply for the following visas to enter Germany:
D visa (national visa) for all longer stays
Must be applied for if your planned stay exceeds 90 days; visas are issued by embassies; D visas can be issued for research purposes for a period of up to 12 months;
Must be applied for if gainful employment is planned;
Processing can take several months (depending on the country);
Issued for different purposes: e.g. study, research, etc.;
Travel within the Schengen area permitted (90 days within 180 days).
A D visa means you can take up gainful employment on the day of entry and apply for a residence permit or to have your visa converted into a residence permit after entry into Germany. While your application for a residence permit is being processed and if required, you can be issued with a provisional residence document in accordance with Section 81(4) of the Residence Act (Aufenthaltsgesetz – AufenthG) to allow re-entry to Germany. This is why it is ALWAYS advisable, even for persons who do not require a visa, to enter Germany on a D visa. Otherwise, only a provisional residence document pursuant to Section 81(3) AufenthG (without the option of re-entry) can be issued.
C visa (Schengen visa, "tourist visa") for all EU & EEA states
Only valid for a maximum stay of 90 days in a period of 180 days. Number of days is documented via entry stamps (you can also use the
Schengen Visa Calculator
to make sure you don’t outstay your visa).
Does not permit gainful employment or (doctoral) studies.
No explicit entitlement to employment; exception: research (see:
Section 5 of the Employment Ordinance (Beschäftigungsverordnung – BeschV)
: An intention to work in research should be stated.
It is not possible to change the purpose of stay and/or extend the duration of stay.
It is best not to apply for a Schengen visa as it is does not allow you to subsequently apply for a residence permit. Once your Schengen visa expires, you have to leave Germany.

Entry Conditions and Visa
Exemptions from the visa requirement
Nationals of the countries listed below are exempt from the visa requirement. They only require a valid travel document (e.g. passport) to enter Germany.
EU states, EEA states:
Nationals can enter the country without a visa and do not require a residence permit for longer stays. However, they must register their new place of residence with the Registration Office/Citizens' Registration Office (Bürgeramt).
Best friend countries
: Nationals of Australia, Israel, Japan, Canada, South Korea, New Zealand, the USA, and the UK, can enter the country without a visa, even if they
plan
to work. A residence permit must be applied for in Germany for
longer stays
(more than 90 days) and
before taking up gainful employment
. It can take up to 12 weeks to apply for a residence permit from the Berlin Immigration Office (LEA).
Please note:
If you are planning to sign an employment contract, we recommend that you apply for a D visa. This entitles you to take up work immediately upon arrival in Germany.
Nationals of Andorra, Brazil, El Salvador, Honduras, Monaco, and San Marino
can also enter without a visa, but are not allowed to work. For a stay of more than 90 days, you will need a residence permit, which can also be applied for without a visa. There are some special regulations for Brazil that may require Brazilian nationals to apply for a visa. Please contact the German diplomatic missions in Brazil for more information.
Please note:
If you are planning to take up gainful employment, you must apply for a visa before entering Germany.
Argentina, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Brunei, Chile, Costa Rica, Guatemala, Malaysia, Mexico, Montenegro, Nicaragua, Panama, Paraguay, Singapore, Serbia, Uruguay, Vatican City, and Venezuela:
Nationals can enter Germany without a visa, but cannot take up gainful employment and
cannot apply for a residence permit
. Nationals of these countries must leave after 90 days.
The general visa requirement applies to nationals of all other countries.

Documents for applying for a visa
Please contact the German embassy or consulate in your home country. You will usually find a list of the documents required on the website of the German embassy. The following documents are usually required when applying for a visa for research work (Section 18d of the Residence Act (Aufenthaltsgesetz - AufenthG)):
Completed application for a visa for the Federal Republic Of Germany
(longer stay, category D visa)
Proof of employment at TU Berlin (confirmation of employment from Human Resources)
Hosting agreement pursuant to Section 18d AufenthG
(prepared by the host institute or the Welcome Center's onboarding service)
Confirmation of doctoral supervision
Proof of livelihood (doctoral studies)
Curriculum vitae and university degree certificates
Proof of health insurance
Passport and up-to-date biometric photo
A translation of the documents into German is recommended.
If accompanied by family members:
Proof of health insurance for all family members
Marriage certificate (with apostille)
Birth certificates of children (with apostille)
Passport and current biometric photo of family members
A translation of the documents into German is recommended

Consular Services Portal
, an easy-to-use and secure digital information and support service for visa processing provided by the

Taking up work with an employment contract (pursuant to Section 18b of the German Residence Act)

Hints and tips
Visas and residence permits are different documents:
A visa only entitles the holder to enter the country. If a longer stay is planned (more than 90 days), a residence permit must be applied for after arrival. Further information can be found on our website under:
Residence Permit
A visa is issued for a specific purpose
, e.g. to take up employment, to study, or to pursue a doctorate. You are restricted to the approved purpose. If you intend to work in Germany, you will generally need a visa issued for this purpose.
If you do not intend to stay in Germany for longer than twelve months,
you can ask the visa office to
issue a visa for the entire duration of your stay
covering all Schengen states (Art. 18(2) of the Schengen Convention). In this case, you do not need to apply for a residence permit in Berlin.
In some cases, German diplomatic missions abroad
issue visas for 12 months
, even though this covers a longer stay (please also refer to the following information on statutory health insurance).
Statutory health insurance for researchers (residence permit issued according to Section 18d AufenthG):
As of 1 March 2024, international visiting scholars issued with a visa before entering Germany covering at least 12 months are entitled to take out voluntary statutory health insurance. You can also apply for statutory health insurance for
family members
(in the case of family reunification) or as
family insurance
in connection with a
visa issued for researchers (Section 18d AufenthG)
. The statutory health insurer issues an insurance declaration from the date of entry; you only have to pay contributions after entry based on the general contribution rate of 14.6% (reduced: 14.0%)
When applying for a visa, please inform the responsible diplomatic mission abroad that,
pursuant to Section 34(2) of the
Ordinance Governing Residence (Aufenthaltsverordnung)
,
you, as a researcher, do not require your visa to be approved
by the Berlin Immigration Office (LEA).
If you are applying for a visa for the
purpose of working as a researcher
with the intention of signing an employment contract in Germany, you can inform the diplomatic mission in your home country that, as a researcher, you do not require your visa to be approved
pursuant to Section 5
of the
Federal Employment Ordinance (Bundesbeschäftigungsverordnung)
. The Federal Employment Agency does not need to be contacted.
Family reunification:
If a researcher enters Germany with a D visa (subsequently converted to a residence permit pursuant to Section 18d AufenthG) and wishes to bring their family to Germany at a later date, the family must enter Germany on a D visa. This is then converted into a residence permit pursuant to Sections 30, 32 et seq. AufenthG. German language skills are not required, but living expenses must be covered along with half of the monthly rental costs.
