Incoming exchanges
Experience life in Canada’s capital while studying at the University of Ottawa. Each year, we welcome hundreds of exchange students from around the world. Join us for one or two academic terms while staying enrolled at your home university, and become part of our vibrant, diverse campus community.
Incoming Exchange Program
Find key facts and important deadlines, step by step guidance to apply and prepare for your exchange, and an overview of the student support services available during your studies.

Exchange students are

must have

How to apply for an exchange
Step 1: Check your eligibility
arrow_drop_down
Before starting the process, make sure you meet
all
eligibility requirements:
You must:
Be registered in an undergraduate or graduate program at your home university. Please note that PhD students are
not eligible
to apply for an exchange.
Have a good academic record:
Minimum
2.7/4.0 GPA
(Canadian system) or
12/20
(French system)
The academic requirement can be higher for graduate-level programs or specific faculties.
Have completed at least one full year of university studies by the time you apply.
Be nominated by your home institution for an exchange at uOttawa.
Submit a complete online exchange application by March 31
(for the Fall term, the Winter term or the full academic year).
Choose courses at uOttawa approved by your home institution.
Meet uOttawa’s language proficiency requirements and upload your test results on time.
Step 2: Demonstrate language proficiency
arrow_drop_down
At uOttawa, you’ll study in English or in French.
If your language of study is
not your mother tongue
, you must submit a language test taken within the last two years.
For accepted tests and scores, see our
language requirements for exchange students
.
Step 3: Prepare and submit your online application
arrow_drop_down
You can only access our online application form once your home university
nominates you for an exchange.
After your nomination is accepted, your home institution will send you a link to the exchange application platform.
You must submit the following documents as part of your application:
Your
official university transcripts
in English or French, along with your institution’s grading scale. If your transcripts are in a language other than English or French, you must provide a scanned copy of the original document and a translation completed by a certified translator.
Official language test results
, if required.
A scanned copy of your
valid passport
.
Your
preliminary course selection
, approved by your home institution. This list reflects the courses you plan to take at uOttawa. It’s considered preliminary because the course schedule for the upcoming year isn’t yet available at the time of application. For more information on preliminary course selection, see Step 4.
Additional documents may be required for:
Common Law
Civil Law
Theatre
Visual Arts
Music
Graduate level applicants

Step 5: Enrol in your courses
arrow_drop_down
Submitting your course list
doesn’t mean
you’re enrolled.
You can only enrol in courses after you’ve been officially accepted into the exchange program. Your acceptance letter will include a course enrolment form and instructions on completing your enrolment.
Course spaces are
not guaranteed

How to prepare for your exchange
Finances and budgeting
arrow_drop_down
As an exchange student, you benefit from special student status, which means you
don’t pay tuition or incidental fees
at uOttawa. However, you might still need to pay for certain
course-related expenses
, such as materials for visual arts.
As well, you’re responsible for
all other costs related to your stay in Canada
, including mandatory health insurance.
When planning your budget, be sure to account for:
Rent or residence fees
Books and supplies
Travel
Daily living expenses
Health insurance
(mandatory for all exchange students)
U-Pass (optional)
(public transit pass for Ottawa and Gatineau, integrated in your uOttawa student card)
To purchase a U‑Pass, submit a request to join the U-Pass program through uoZone and see the associated fees.

Immigration and travel documents
arrow_drop_down
If you’re coming to uOttawa for
one term
, you don’t have to apply for a study permit, although you may choose to do so. If you enter Canada as a visitor (without a study permit), you
won’t be allowed to work
during your exchange.
If your exchange will last
two terms (more than six months)
, you
must apply for a study permit
before arriving in Canada. Make sure to check the
Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) processing times
and
required documents
well in advance, to avoid delays.
Most international students need an entry document to travel to Canada. Depending on your citizenship, you will require either a temporary resident visa (TRV) or an electronic travel authorization (eTA). Learn more about
entry requirements by country or territory
.
Once you receive your acceptance letter, our
Student Immigration Advising team
will be happy to help you with any immigration-related questions you may have. You’re also welcome to join one of our weekly
virtual immigration drop-in sessions
, where you can get answers and guidance during the session.
Housing
arrow_drop_down
Finding a place to live is an important part of planning your exchange.
On-campus housing isn’t guaranteed for exchange students
, and only a limited number of residence spaces become available each year.
Once you’re officially accepted into the exchange program, you’ll receive access to our Housing Portal, where you can apply for on-campus accommodation if spaces are still available. You can also email the Housing Service to inquire about current available on-campus housing at
[email protected]
.
Because residence space is limited, we strongly recommend exploring off‑campus housing options. Many uOttawa students (including exchange students) live in the Sandy Hill neighbourhood, which is right next to campus and offers a variety of housing options.
For guidance, resources and tips on renting in Ottawa, see our
off-campus housing information and resources
. If you need additional support, you can email the Off‑Campus Housing team at
[email protected]
.
Health insurance
arrow_drop_down
All international exchange students
must
be covered by the
University Health Insurance Plan (UHIP)
during their stay at uOttawa. Once you enrol in your courses, the University will
automatically
sign you up for UHIP. This plan covers essential medical services, such as doctor or hospital visits, surgeries and medical tests.
Your UHIP fees will appear on your statement of account in uoZone (our virtual student portal), and you can pay them using any of the
accepted payment methods
. After the University receives your payment, the insurance provider, Cowan, will email a PDF copy of your
UHIP coverage card
to your uOttawa email account within
10 business days
. Don’t forget to check your spam folder to make sure you don’t miss this important document.
Because UHIP covers only certain medical services, you can purchase
additional health and dental insurance
. This optional coverage can help pay for services not covered by UHIP, such as prescription medications, dental care, optometry care and services from certain specialists.

Virtual orientation sessions
arrow_drop_down
In the summer, you’ll receive an invitation to join our
virtual orientation sessions
for new students. These sessions cover important topics like immigration, health insurance and getting ready to start your studies. Although they’re designed for students completing their entire degree at uOttawa, the information is very helpful for exchange students as well. Our expert teams will be there to answer your questions and provide guidance tailored to your exchange experience.
Peer mentoring
arrow_drop_down
Starting your exchange at uOttawa can be exciting, but also a big adjustment. Our
peer mentoring team
(a group of friendly student mentors from around the world) is here to help you feel supported. Whether you’re new to Canadian university life, feeling a bit homesick or unsure about the support services available on campus, you can reach out to talk things through with someone who understands. Mentors can also help you get involved on campus, learn more about life in Ottawa and connect you with support services across the University.

What happens when you arrive at uOttawa
Orientation Day
The Student Mobility team offers an Orientation Day (registration fee required) to welcome you as a new exchange student and help you get comfortable on campus. It’s a great way to get to know your surroundings, meet other students and take part in activities that will help you start your term with confidence.
We’ll email you details closer to the day.

Receiving your transcript at the end of your exchange
At the end of your exchange, the Student Mobility team will email you your official uOttawa transcript at no cost. If you complete your exchange during the fall term, you’ll receive your transcript by mid-February. If you complete your exchange during the winter term or over the full academic year, you’ll receive it by the end of May.
Make sure you don’t owe any fees to the University (such as health insurance fees), as outstanding charges will prevent your transcript from being issued.
Paid exchange program
You have two options to complete a student exchange at the University of Ottawa: with or without a student mobility agreement.
To check if there’s an agreement between your university and uOttawa, see our
list of partner universities
. If your university isn’t on the list, you can still apply through the paid exchange program.
Main differences:
The paid exchange program is
only for undergraduate students
.
You must pay the applicable uOttawa tuition fees.
You are billed for your official transcript at the end of your paid exchange.

.
