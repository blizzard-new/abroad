CSU International Programs (CSU IP) 
Campus and Course Selection Guidelines for Spring 2027  
 
 
California State University (CSU) is the country’s largest four-year university system with 22 campuses located throughout 
the state of California offering bachelor’s and master’s degree programs in over 350 subject areas. All 22 campuses accept 
exchange students. However, some campuses are more competitive than others, and some subjects have limited or no 
availability for exchange students. 
 
Read this document carefully before you apply. 
 
Meeting minimum eligibility allows your application to be reviewed. It does not guarantee placement at any campus. 
 
Your application will be reviewed based on: 
• Academic preparation 
• Academic feasibility 
• Campus competitiveness 
• Available capacity 
• Complete and timely submission 
 
1. Campus Competitiveness 
 
CSU campuses are grouped by general competitiveness. 
 
Green Group (More Placement Opportunities) 
Bakersfield, Chico, Dominguez Hills, East Bay, Fresno, Humboldt, Monterey Bay, Northridge, San Bernardino, San 
Francisco, San Marcos, Sonoma, Stanislaus 
 
These campuses generally offer the greatest number of placement opportunities and a wider range of available courses 
for exchange students. 
 
Blue Group (Moderate Competition) 
Channel Islands, Fullerton, Maritime Academy, Pomona, Sacramento, San José, San Luis Obispo 
 
These campuses receive moderate demand and may have more limited placement opportunities in some subject areas. 
 
Red Group (Highly Competitive) 
Long Beach, Los Angeles, San Diego 
 
These campuses receive the highest number of applications and have the most limited placement opportunities. 
Admission is highly competitive. 
 
Important Rules 
• You may list only one Red Group campus. 
• If you list a Red Group campus, you must list four campuses total. 
• Competitive campuses require a complete application by the deadline. This means there cannot be any missing 
items or incomplete fields.  
 
Choosing campuses from different groups (Green, Blue, and Red) gives you more placement opportunities. Many Green 
Group campuses also offer excellent academic programs and a broad selection of courses across many subject areas.

2. How Many Campuses to List 
 
The number of campuses you must list depends on your first-choice campus and the subjects you wish to study. 
 
You must list: 
• At least two campuses if your first-choice campus is in the Green Group.  
• At least three campuses if your first-choice campus is in the Blue Group.  
• Four campuses if:  
o You list a Red Group campus, or  
o You wish to study a restricted subject area at any campus.  
 
We strongly encourage you to include at least one Green Group campus if it offers your subject area. Green Group 
campuses generally provide more placement opportunities and often offer a wider range of courses available to 
exchange students. 
 
Choosing campuses from a mix of Green, Blue, and Red Groups gives you more placement options and may improve 
your chances of receiving a placement. 
 
Only list campuses where you would be happy to study. If you are offered a placement at one of the campuses on your 
application, you should be prepared to accept that placement. 
 
3. Choosing Courses (Subjects) 
 
For each campus on your application, you must list eight subjects (called "courses" at the CSU) in priority order. 
 
Applications with fewer than eight courses for each campus are incomplete. Incomplete applications are not eligible for 
review for Red Group campuses. If time permits, CSU IP may ask you to provide additional course choices so your 
application can be considered for other campuses. 
 
When choosing courses: 
• Select courses that support your degree program and academic goals.  
• Make sure you have completed the required prerequisites for every course you list. Course prerequisites can be 
found in the campus's online Course Catalog (or Bulletin) under the course descriptions. 
• Choose courses that match your academic level. Upper-division courses are intended for students who have 
already completed introductory university-level study in that subject.  
• Include alternative course options. If you list courses in a restricted subject area, you must also list an equal 
number of courses from non-restricted subject areas. 
 
Do not: 
• List courses in subject areas that are impacted (closed) to exchange students.  
• List courses for which you do not meet the prerequisites.  
• List only restricted courses.  
• Assume that listing a course guarantees you will be able to enroll in it.  
 
Important 
Applicants who list only restricted courses, or who do not include an equal number of non-restricted courses, will not 
be considered for placement at Red Group campuses. This requirement helps ensure that students can build a full-time 
study program if access to restricted courses is limited. 
 
Final course registration is determined by the host campus and depends on: 
• Your academic preparation

• Completion of course prerequisites  
• Department approval (where required)  
• Course availability  
• Campus restrictions  
 
Being flexible when selecting courses gives you more opportunities to build a full-time study program after arrival. 
 
4. Impacted and Restricted Subjects 
 
Some subject areas have enrollment limitations for exchange students. Before selecting your campuses and courses, 
review the Impacted (Closed) and Restricted Subject Areas tables in this guide. 
 
Impacted (Closed) 
These subject areas are not available to exchange students. 
 
Do not list a campus if the subject area you plan to study is listed as Impacted (Closed). 
 
If you do, that campus cannot be considered for placement. 
 
Restricted (Limited) 
These subject areas accept only a limited number of exchange students. Places are competitive and cannot be 
guaranteed. 
 
If you choose a campus where your subject area is listed as Restricted, you must: 
• List an equal number of courses from non-restricted subject areas.  
• Be prepared to take courses outside the restricted subject area if access is limited.  
• Include additional campus choices on your application.  
 
Applicants who do not include an equal number of non-restricted courses will not be considered for placement at Red 
Group campuses. 
 
Important  
The following courses are not available to exchange students at any CSU campus: 
• Extended Education courses  
• Certificate programs  
• Credential programs  
• Nursing programs 
• Graduate-level business courses 
 
Host campuses determine which subject areas are impacted or restricted. These designations may change without 
notice, including after you submit your application. Final course availability is determined by the host campus.

To study Business at the CSU, you must: 
1. Be enrolled in a business-related degree at your home university.  
2. Have successfully completed at least one semester of university-level business coursework.  
3. Meet the prerequisites for all courses listed on your application.  
4. Comply with campus-specific restrictions.  
 
Preparing for Upper-Division Business Courses 
Most exchange students who study Business enroll primarily in upper-division courses.  Before arriving at the CSU, you 
should have successfully completed introductory university-level coursework in: 
• Accounting  
• Economics  
• Mathematics  
• Statistics  
• The business discipline you plan to study (for example, Finance, Marketing, Management, or Information 
Systems)  
 
Upper-division courses assume you already have this academic background. 
 
Students who do not have the required preparation may not be eligible to enroll in upper-division business courses. 
 
If You Apply to a Campus Where Business is Restricted 
If Business is listed as Restricted at a campus: 
• You must list an equal number of courses from non-business subject areas.  
• You should be prepared to take courses outside Business if business courses are full.  
• You should include additional campus choices on your application.  
 
Applicants who do not include an equal number of non-business courses will not be considered for placement at Red 
Group campuses where Business is restricted.

7. Graduate (Postgraduate) Students 
 
Postgraduate students are welcome to apply for exchange. 
 
In most cases, exchange placements are at the undergraduate level. Postgraduate exchange placements are very 
limited. 
 
Postgraduate students must: 
• Be willing to accept an undergraduate placement.  
• Select undergraduate courses on their application.  
• Be flexible in their campus choices.  
 
In very limited cases, graduate-level courses may be available, depending on the campus, subject area, available places, 
and host campus approval. Postgraduate students may also be required to meet additional requirements set by the host 
campus. 
 
Postgraduate placements and access to graduate-level courses cannot be guaranteed. 
 
Graduate-level business courses are not available to exchange students. Postgraduate students studying business are 
welcome to apply for exchange but must be willing to accept an undergraduate placement and enroll only in 
undergraduate business courses.

9. Academic Feasibility 
 
Before a campus placement can be made, CSU IP reviews whether the courses you selected are an appropriate match 
for your academic background, previous studies, and degree program. This is called academic feasibility. 
 
To support a successful academic feasibility review, you should: 
• Select courses that match your degree program and academic background.  
• Meet the prerequisites for all courses listed on your application.  
• Choose courses that are appropriate for your academic level.  
• Demonstrate sufficient preparation for upper-division courses, if requested.  
• Comply with any campus restrictions for your subject area.  
 
For campuses where the number of qualified applicants exceeds the number of available places (Red Group campuses), 
applicants who meet the minimum eligibility requirements are compared on the overall strength, consistency, and level 
of preparation demonstrated by their academic record for the requested coursework. 
 
If your selected courses are not an appropriate academic match, your application may not be considered for that 
campus, even if you meet the minimum GPA requirement. 
 
Meeting the minimum eligibility requirements does not guarantee placement. 
 
10. Deadlines and Complete Applications 
 
Applications for Red Group campuses must be complete by the CSU IP application deadline published on our website. 
 
A complete application includes: 
• Official transcripts from all post-secondary institutions attended.  
• English proficiency documentation (if required).  
• Financial documentation.  
• All required forms and signatures.  
• Required portfolios, work samples, or other major-specific materials (if applicable).  
• Completed application fields.  
• Eight courses listed for each campus selected.  
 
Applications that are late or incomplete by the published deadline will not be considered for Red Group campuses. 
 
If host campus deadlines permit, late or incomplete applications may be considered for Blue Group or Green Group 
campuses once all required documents have been received. 
 
Submitting a complete application by the deadline gives you the greatest number of placement opportunities.

11. If You Are Not Placed at Your First-Choice Campus 
 
If you cannot be placed at your first-choice campus, CSU IP will review your remaining campus choices in the order you 
listed them. 
 
Placement at each campus depends on: 
• Meeting the campus eligibility requirements.  
• Academic feasibility for the courses requested.  
• Campus competitiveness.  
• Available places at the time your application is reviewed.  
 
If you are not eligible for placement at any of the campuses listed on your application, or if no places remain available, 
CSU IP may discuss alternative campus options with you or your home university exchange coordinator. 
 
Only list campuses where you would be happy to study. Every campus listed on your application should offer courses 
that support your degree program and academic goals. 
 
Listing a campus on your application does not guarantee placement at that campus. 
 
Core Principle 
 
CSU IP seeks to place students at the campus that provides the strongest opportunity for academic success and 
meaningful progress toward their degree. 
 
Campus placement is based on: 
1. Academic preparation  
2. Academic feasibility  
3. Campus competitiveness (where applicable)  
4. Available places  
5. A complete application submitted by the published deadline  
 
Meeting the minimum eligibility requirements allows your application to be considered. It does not guarantee 
placement at a particular campus. 
 
Applicants who carefully select campuses, choose appropriate courses, and submit a complete application by the 
deadline generally have the greatest placement opportunities.

13. Using the Campus Subject Area Tables 
Before selecting your campuses, use the tables on the following pages to check whether your subject area is Open, 
Restricted (Limited), or Impacted (Closed) at each CSU campus. 
 
To find campuses that offer your subject area, visit degrees.calstate.edu. This website is designed to help you identify 
CSU campuses that offer your subject area. Do not use this website to apply for exchange. 
 
Once you have identified possible campus choices: 
• Visit each campus website to review course descriptions and prerequisites.  
• Use the tables on the following pages to check whether your subject area is Open, Restricted (Limited), or 
Impacted (Closed).  
• Select courses for which you meet the prerequisites.  
 
When using the tables: 
• Open – Exchange students may apply for these subject areas, subject to prerequisites and course availability.  
• Restricted (Limited) – Only a limited number of exchange students can be accommodated. If you select a 
restricted subject area, you must also list an equal number of courses from non-restricted subject areas.  
• Impacted (Closed) – These subject areas are not available to exchange students. Do not list these campuses if 
you intend to study that subject area.
