What are ECTS credits, and how many hours of work do they entail?
What are ECTS credits?
ECTS is short for European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System. It is a standard way to compare the credits associated with a certain amount of learning and the associated workload in higher education across the European Union.
Workload
ECTS is a credit system based on student workload. The credits are a value given to course units to describe the amount of work needed to complete them successfully.
1 ECTS corresponds to 25 to 30 hours of work
per semester (including time spent in class, meetings for group work, reading assignments, studying for exams, etc.).
ECTS credits express a relative value. Study hours are estimates because you may spend more time on a course you are not so familiar with and maybe less on another course that is exactly in your area of interest and expertise. That means that one of your 3 ECTS courses may involve more work than a 6 ECTS course.
A bachelor's degree consists of 180 ECTS credits. One year’s study is 60 credits, which is normally equivalent to 1500–1800 hours of total workload.

Which type of visa do I need?
If you need a visa to study at Thomas More, you will need to apply for a long-stay visa (type D). This is also called an Authorisation for Provisional Sojourn (Machtiging tot Voorlopig Verblijf). It allows the holder to enter and remain in Belgium for a period longer than 90 days.
Students should not enter Belgium on a tourist visa if they intend to stay longer than 90 days. Thomas More will not support an application for a change of status or visa.
Where should I submit my visa application?
The visa application must be completed at the
Belgian Consulate or Embassy
in your home country or in the country where you legally reside. If there is no Belgian consulate or embassy in your country, you may submit your application to the
Belgian consulate or Embassy
in a neighbouring country.  In some countries, a visa application centre handles the visa procedures for Belgium, for example
VFS Global
and
TLS
.
Make sure that you inform Thomas More via email (Degree students:
admissions@thomasmore.be
. Exchange students:
incoming@thomasmore.be
) about the application number and/or contact person at the Belgian embassy or consulate where you are applying for your visa. This way, we can confirm your admission directly with them.
When should I start the visa application?
If you need a visa, we highly recommend starting this process as soon as you have received the necessary documents from Admissions (for Degree students) or International Office (for Exchange students). Student visa applications can often take 3 to 4 months to be processed so it is essential to start the process as soon as you received your documents.
Plan to be in Belgium at least two weeks before the start of the classes in order to attend
Orientation Days
.
It is important to refer to the Belgian Embassy or Belgian Consulate website in your home or neighbouring country. They will provide you with information about which documents are required to apply for a student visa. Please note that some embassies and consulates work with external agencies like VFS or TLS.

Important additional requirements for a student visa
Please take these additional tips & tricks into account when applying for your student visa:
The Belgian Embassy will ask you to fill out a questionnaire or they will have an interview. In this interview they will assess your
study choice, university choice, post-study plans
etc... It is of utmost importance that you prepare yourself for these questions and that you have thoroughly studied your programme and Thomas More. Attending our
webinars
can be a great start, but also checking out our website. The Embassy will expect you to know exact details of your programme and studies and well thought through answers (no general, vague answers).
Please make sure to not share your answers with fellow student visa applicants. The Embassy will notice and this will most likely have a negative effect on your visa decision.
Examples of questions that you could get asked:
What kind of studies do you want to follow in Belgium?
What motivated your choice for these kind of studies?
Give a brief survey of the programme of the studies you want to follow.
Which profession would you like to exercise?
Which are the professional outlets in your country of origin with the diploma you will have obtained in Belgium?
How does the blocked account procedure work for exchange students?
Thomas More offers exchange students who have been accepted to one of their programmes the option of a blocked account procedure.
What is a blocked account procedure?
It's a way for you to prove your financial solvency, i.e. sufficient means of subsistence, when applying for a student visa.
How does it work?
You make an
advance payment
of your monthly living expenses into a blocked account managed by Thomas More. The minimum amount to cover your living expenses is €1062 per month and is set by the Belgian government*. There is an administrative fee of €250.
Blocked account 26/27:  €1062 x 5 months + 250€ administrative fee =  €5560
When you arrive in Belgium you
open a Belgian bank account
in your name into which you receive monthly payments during your stay with us.  At the end of your stay you are fully reimbursed, apart from the €250 administrative fee.
* Be aware that the monthly amount is set by the Belgian Government and is subject to change at any given time. If any changes should occur we will notify our students.
How can I apply for a blocked account?
If you want to apply for a blocked account as an exchange student, you need to contact the International Office yourself asap via
incoming@thomasmore.be
. They will send you the procedure via email.
Please note you should do this as soon as possible, since it takes some time to set it up.
Should your visa be refused, the blocked account will be fully refunded (€-250 administrative fee 26/27) onto the account where the money originally was transferred from.
