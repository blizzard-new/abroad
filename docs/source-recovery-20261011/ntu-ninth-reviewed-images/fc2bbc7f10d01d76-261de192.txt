About SFU 
 
Simon Fraser University (SFU) is a globally engaged institution known for excellence in research, 
innovation, and sustainability. Ranked among Canada’s top comprehensive universities SFU is a leader 
in interdisciplinary education, offering programs that integrate diverse fields of study to foster innovation, 
critical thinking, and global collaboration.  
Born in 1965, SFU has grown into a leading institution with vibrant campuses in British Columbia's largest 
municipalities — Vancouver, Burnaby and Surrey — and deep roots in partner communities throughout 
the province and around the world.

Course Selection 
 
 
At the time of application, students will list their preferred courses or research 
proposal (for graduate students planning to pursue research at SFU) on their 
online application.  
Undergraduate students must submit a minimum of 8 courses (they will take 
3-5 courses). Undergraduate courses are numbered 100-400. Undergraduate 
students may take courses from multiple subject areas (Students coming to 
SFU from a Business partner are required to take a minimum of 3 courses from 
within the Beedie School of Business.). 
 
Graduate students must submit a minimum of 3 courses from within the same 
department (unless they will be doing full-time research). Graduate courses are 
numbered 500-900. Graduate students can take courses from within a single 
subject area. 
SFU does its best to accommodate course requests, but cannot guarantee 
access to specific courses. Students must consider each course’ SFU 
restrictions and prerequisites at time of application to determine if a course is 
suitable for them to take. For more information on course selection, to see 
courses lists and course outlines please see: 
• Academic Information 
• Restrictions and Considerations 
 
Course Enrollment

Term Dates 
 
SFU operates on a unique trimester system, comprised of 3 equal full-time terms per year. Exchange and 
study abroad students are welcome to start their exchange in the Fall or Spring term (normally for either 1 
or 2 terms). The Fall and Spring terms are the busiest on campus, and Summer term has approximately 
80% of regular courses offerings. For actual term dates visit Academic Calendar.

Degree Level  
 
SFU welcomes both undergraduate and graduate (Masters or PhD) exchange students, 
though graduate options may not be possible in all academic areas in all terms and are 
always evaluated on a case-by-case basis by the academic unit. Reference our 
exchange agreement to determine which levels are open for your students. Most 
exchange/study abroad students are registered as undergraduate students, even when 
they are graduate students at their home institution. See the additional note about 
graduate acceptance in the “Acceptance” section below. 
If exchange students want to study at the graduate level, they are recommended to 
explore their preferred host program/faculty/department and their course offerings per 
term to see if they align with their current study in their home university, then connect 
with exchange@sfu.ca in advance of their nomination to discuss the possibility. Without 
proper academic backgrounds, their admission request could be denied by the 
program/faculty/department. See the list of Graduate Studies Programs and their 
academic levels offered at SFU.   
Currently, the Beedie School of Business and Department of Psychology do not accept 
exchange students to the graduate programs, but this list is subject to change.  
 
Degree Progress 
 
SFU does not require exchange and study abroad students to have completed a 
minimum number of credits, courses or years prior to their exchange.

Acceptance 
 
When students submit a complete application by the stated deadline, they will receive 
their SFU Admission Letter as follows: 
 
Fall Admission: by end of May 
Spring Admission: by end of October 
 
Students will receive a pdf copy of their admission letter from ISS which can be printed. 
Partners will be copied on this email. We no longer issue hard copy acceptance letters 
except in unique circumstances. 
 
Graduate applications may take additional time to process. Graduate admission for 
exchange students is based on student eligibility and also on departmental/faculty 
capacity. Applicants will be reviewed on an individual basis by their prospective host 
department/faculty. Graduate admission cannot be guaranteed. A student who cannot be 
offered graduate admission may be admitted as undergraduate if desired, provided they 
meet the admission requirements.

Provincial Attestation Letters (PAL) (The information below is current as of September 2025 
and is subject to change without notice.): 
On January 22, 2025 Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) announced 
that students are exempt from the PAL if "you’re an exchange student studying under an 
exchange arrangement between your home institution and a designated learning institution 
(DLI) in Canada, and you don’t pay tuition fees to the DLI." This means that two-term 
exchange students who require a study permit and who are not paying tuition to SFU, 
will no longer need a PAL to come on exchange to SFU. Exchange students applying for 
a study permit will be required to provide proof of their exemption. SFU will work with your 
students to provide them with the documentation needed. 
 
However, the PAL does still apply to study abroad students who are paying their tuition to 
SFU. We will be in touch with partners directly who this applies to.

Services 
Academic Accommodation: 
Students seeking academic accommodations should contact SFU’s Centre for Accessible

All incoming exchange / study abroad students have access to SFU’s Health and

My Student Support Program (My SSP): 
My SSP is a resource designed to support the overall mental health and well-being of 
students by providing real-time 24/7 access to professional counsellors, with multiple 
language options.  
Student Learning Commons (SLC): 
SFU’s Student Learning Commons provides expert and friendly help with academic writing, 
learning and study strategies in an environment of collaboration, discussion and peer 
learning, and various academic workshop at no cost.
